## Discussion

The Three Guiding Moves work together to create and restore coherence. **Establish the Circle** creates a recurring space in which different perspectives become visible and shared understanding can develop. **Integrate the Conversation** helps that space remain generative when tension, competing priorities, or differences in interpretation threaten to fragment it. **Reconcile the Intention** carries emerging understanding forward through recurring conversation around a visible artifact that can be examined, affirmed, and adapted over time. Together, the Moves establish a place for coherence to form, integrate what emerges there, and make shared understanding usable for coordinated action.

The Moves are not a linear process. Action changes conditions, information emerges, and relationships shift, so what once seemed coherent may need to be revisited. Practitioners can combine and adapt the Moves as circumstances change.

Circle3 can be understood as operating at the **human layer of change**: the shared interpretations, relationships, conversations, and intentions through which people make sense and coordinate action. This layer interacts with, but is distinct from, the formal structures used to organize change. Governance establishes authority and decision rights; program management coordinates work; organizational design establishes roles and structures; and technical or operational practices address specialized problems. Circle3 does not replace these approaches. It works on the human conditions through which people use them coherently.

Facilitation is an important capacity for applying the Moves. Establish the Circle calls for integrative facilitation to help different perspectives develop shared meaning; Integrate the Conversation makes this capacity especially explicit by working directly with attention, participation, tension, and conversational flow; and Reconcile the Intention depends on structured dialogue that can surface and reconcile differing interpretations. The Moves therefore assume sufficient facilitation capability is present, whether it resides in a dedicated facilitator or is shared among participants. Developing this capability is outside the scope of this paper.

Future work has two immediate directions. First, continued use across organizational and community contexts can refine the existing Moves and Leadership Lenses, particularly their forces, failure modes, relationships, and boundaries. Second, because integrative facilitation is fundamental to the Moves, supporting patterns may be mined that make this capacity more explicit and distributed, allowing participants to take greater responsibility for maintaining coherent conversation together.
